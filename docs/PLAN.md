# Plan: one path from zero to A2

Status: agreed with the owner on 2026-11-24 (decisions at the end); built in the order under "How it is built", one commit per piece, each recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## What A1 and A2 mean here

- **Topics and goals:** the Ministry of Education's curriculum *Íslenska fyrir útlendinga – grunnnám* (2008) has four stages of 60 teaching hours that "train the skills that correspond to A1–A2" of the Common European Framework. The plan reads stages 1–2 as A1 and stages 3–4 as A2 (the curriculum does not split them by level; that is this plan's reading).
  - Stage 1: name, address, politeness, the alphabet, numbers, age, work, the clock, working and school hours, today and yesterday, days and months, sleep and washing, meals, food, buying, how you feel, the body.
  - Stage 2: hobbies, appearance, clothes, colors, the school and work day, where things are, cooking and the table, describing people and homes, ordering, tickets, sending and fetching, the bank, weather and road conditions; talking simply about the past; forms and short messages.
  - Stage 3: housing and rent, emergencies (112, police, fire, ambulance, the doctor), holidays and customs, plans for the future, feelings, praising and complaining.
  - Stage 4: media, school and children, the workplace, job applications and payslips, travel and geography.
- **Grammar:** elon.io's A1 path (pronunciation, gender, the article, present tense, questions, numbers to 20, basic agreement and prepositions) and A2 path (all four cases, strong and weak nouns and the plural, the article in full, strong and weak adjectives and comparison, the past of weak and strong verbs, the perfect, motion and place after two-case prepositions, dative and accusative subjects, dates and ordinals). Its list of what a learner should do before B1 (any known noun in all four cases, singular and plural, with and without the article; strong or weak adjectives; the past and the perfect of common verbs; the case after two-case prepositions; dative and accusative subjects) becomes the grammar part of the A2 check.
- **Size:** about 800 words for A1 and 1,600 in all for A2 (Duolingo's figure of about 800 words per level; no Icelandic source gives a count). TÖLVA had 337 when the plan was made, 360 with *Um mig*.

## The path: one path, two sections

One continuous path from zero, as now, divided into sections like Duolingo's:

- **Section A1** (units 1–20) and **section A2** (units 21–36). Each section has a banner with its level, its goals (*"can do X"*), and progress ("A1: 412 of 800 words, 9 of 15 goals").
- **A checkpoint ends each section**: the A1 check (unit 20) and the A2 check (unit 36), scored per goal. Finishing it opens the next section, like a unit review opens the next unit now. A learner who misses goals is shown which units train them.
- **Jump to a section:** at the top of section A2, "Already know A1? Take the A1 check" skips section A1 when passed (the placement test's machinery). The placement test from the first page still places within a section.
- **Spiral:** A2 comes back to A1's themes with more (food: shopping in A1, cooking and complaining in A2; past tense: *var*, *fór* in A1, every verb in A2).
- **Bonus:** unit 10 now (*Nýyrði*, new words from old roots) is split: its five everyday words move to the units that need them (*farsími* and *tölvupóstur* to *Um mig*, *sjónvarp* to *Heima*, *flugvél* to *Gata*, *útvarp* to *Fréttir*), and the unit, with its guidebook on how Icelandic builds words, Katla's story and the seven rarer words (*forrit*, *hátalari*, *þota*, *tækni*, *rafmagn*, *veira*, *reikistjarna*), becomes a bonus after section A2.
- **Letters and sounds,** always open beside the path (see below), like Duolingo's character practice for Japanese: every letter, its sound, how it differs from Norwegian, and short drills to come back to.
- Later sections (B1) can follow the same way; elon.io has a B1 path.

## Letters and sounds (*Stafirnir*)

Asked by the owner: an area to learn Icelandic's letters and their sounds against Norwegian, always open, like Duolingo's character practice for Japanese. For a Norwegian the problem is less new shapes than familiar shapes with other sounds.

- **The alphabet:** all 32 letters in Icelandic order (*a á b d ð e é f g h i í j k l m n o ó p r s t u ú v x y ý þ æ ö*), each with its name, its sound (IPA) and example words with audio (human recordings first). *c*, *q*, *w* and *z* appear only in names and foreign words. The order matters in a dictionary: *á* comes after *a*, *ð* after *d*, and *þ*, *æ*, *ö* at the end.
- **Three groups against Norwegian:**
  - new letters: *þ* and *ð*;
  - familiar shapes with another sound: *æ* (like "ai"), *á* ("au"), *ó* ("ou"), *é* ("je"), *ö* (close to Norwegian *ø*), *u*, *y* and *ý* (no rounded "y" sound), *au* (like Norwegian *øy*), *ei* and *ey* (like Norwegian *ei*);
  - combinations: *hv* ("kv"), *ll* ("tl"), *f* between vowels ("v"), *pp*, *tt*, *kk* (a breath before the stop), long and short vowels.
  Every one of these claims is checked against published sources (Wikipedia's Icelandic and Norwegian phonology, Wiktionary) and listed in `content/CLAIMS.md` before it is shown.
- **Drills,** about ten items, to come back to any time: hear a word and pick the letter or combination in it (*ó* or *o*, *ð* or *d*, *hv* or *v*); same or different from Norwegian; spell a short word you hear; the sound pairs that exist now (vowel length, the breath before *pp*/*tt*/*kk*, *ll*). The special-letters typing drill moves here.
- **Mastery per letter:** each letter is a spaced-repetition card (`letter|æ`), so the grid shows a ring per letter, weak letters come back in reviews, and "practice weak letters" picks them.
- **Where:** in the main navigation (a sixth tab if it fits a 320 px phone, otherwise at the top of Practice), and offered once at the start of the path ("Start with the letters, five minutes"), never required.

## New exercise types

- **Numbers by ear** (A1): hear a price, a time or a phone number and pick or type it.
- **Guided writing** (A1, A2): fill in a form about yourself, write a short message or email from a model, graded in the browser by slot patterns (any name, a number word, a place after *í* in the dative).
- **Past and perfect** (A2): the verb-form exercises (`conjugate`, `typeform`) extended to the supine and the perfect; **principal parts** of strong verbs as a table (*drekka, drakk, drukku, drukkið*).
- **Plural and full tables** in lessons (A2): the table exercise already shows any part of a table in reviews.
- **Reading and listening questions** (A2): a story or dialogue with questions about it (questions in English and Norwegian).
- **Section checks**: tasks drawn from the section's goals, scored per goal.

## How it is built

1. **Foundations** (before any new unit):
   - sections in `content/units/index.json`, banners, checkpoints and "jump to" tests on the Learn page;
   - finished units recorded in progress, so reordering units or adding lessons never locks a learner out;
   - the goal map (`content/goals.json`: each goal, its source and the units and exercises that train it) and level progress on Profile;
   - a word picker script (topic, frequency, sentences opened up) that lists candidates per unit;
   - data loaded per section, so a beginner's phone does not load A2's paradigms and sentences, and the precache follows the learner's section.
2. **Letters and sounds** (*Stafirnir*), before the new units, so learners have it from the start.
3. **Section A1**: reorder the existing units and split unit 10, then the new units 2, 5, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18 one at a time, the extra lessons in units 7 and 16, numbers by ear, guided writing, the A1 check.
4. **Section A2**: the A2 exercise types, then units 21–35 one at a time, the A2 check.
5. **Each unit**, as units 3–10 were made: choose the words; check them in the DMII Core, Wiktionary and Bokmålsordboka; build the data, lint and tag the sentences; group the lessons by hand and read every generated exercise; write the guidebook and the story with every claim sourced in `content/CLAIMS.md`; translate the new sentences into Bokmål by hand; render audio in four voices; run the tests and the visual QA; commit and push.

About 42 commits: 5 foundations, 2 for letters and sounds, 13 for section A1, 4 for A2's exercise types, 16 for section A2, 2 for the jump tests and Profile.

## Costs

- **Bokmål translations by hand:** about 3,000 sentences for A1 and 2,000 more for A2 (measured: a cap of 25 sentences per word would save only about 7%, see decision 4).
- **Audio:** about 45 MB now; about 130 MB for A1 and 180 MB for A2 in four voices (a sentence is about 6 KB per voice; fetched only when played, but in the repository).
- **Data per page:** the lesson page loads all course data (1.3 MB now); without loading per section it would be roughly 5 MB at A2 (an estimate from the growth in words and sentences).
- **Limits:** an app trains understanding, reading and writing well; spoken conversation only partly (speaking practice stays experimental and depends on the browser).

## Decisions

Decided by the owner (2026-11-24):

1. **One path with sections.**
2. **The existing units are reordered** to put the new ones where they belong; recorded progress keeps a learner's finished units finished.
3. **Thin topics and everyday texts:** sentences made from a real Tatoeba sentence by putting in another word in its DMII form (*Ég fæddist í maí* → *í janúar*), and for A2's reading tasks short everyday texts written for the course (an ad, a message, a form); all checked by GreynirCorrect and the parser and listed in `content/CLAIMS.md`.

4. **Every usable sentence is kept, with audio in all four voices** (no cap per word). Measured: at A2 size about 6,400 sentences; at most 25 per word would keep about 5,950 (7% fewer) and at most 10 about 4,800, and a cap mostly drops the sentences made only of common words, the easiest ones for beginners.
5. **Unit 10 is split:** its five everyday words (*sjónvarp*, *útvarp*, *farsími*, *tölvupóstur*, *flugvél*) move to the units that need them, and the rest becomes a bonus after section A2.
6. **Letters and sounds** (*Stafirnir*) is added, always open beside the path.
